How to find affordable therapy in Ontario

Choosing a therapist involves both the cost and the person you’ll be talking to. These questions can help you compare options without having to share your whole story at the first enquiry.

By My Safe Zone, a program of Pneuma Health Inc. · Updated November 16, 2026

Compare the full cost

Ask for the session fee, session length, cancellation policy and any other charges before booking. If you are comparing several providers, write down the same details for each one. A lower price for a shorter appointment is a different offer.

At My Safe Zone, a 60-minute session costs $99 CAD. Two sessions would cost $198 and four would cost $396 before any insurance reimbursement. These are budgeting examples, not a recommendation for how often to attend; discuss that with your therapist.

If a fee is outside your budget, ask the provider whether they offer a reduced-fee option and whether there is a waiting list. You can also explore community services through 211 Ontario. We do not guarantee that another service will be free or available, and My Safe Zone’s published price remains $99 per session.

Check the person’s professional registration

Read the clinician’s full name and designation on their profile. For Ontario providers, you can check a psychotherapist through the College of Registered Psychotherapists of Ontario’s public register or a social worker through the Ontario College of Social Workers and Social Service Workers’ online register. Use the regulator’s current record rather than relying only on a directory badge.

Ask your insurer before you book

If you have benefits, ask your insurer whether your plan covers the specific clinician’s designation, what your remaining annual allowance is, and whether there is a per-session limit. Ask the clinic what information will appear on the receipt and whether you need to pay upfront.

My Safe Zone receipts are issued under Pneuma Health. Some plans may allow direct billing, but coverage, reimbursement and direct billing are not guaranteed.

Make a short list of what matters to you

You might want a particular language, an appointment that fits around work, experience with a concern you want to discuss, or an approach that feels comfortable. You do not need a diagnosis to ask a provider about their experience and services.

  • Do you work with people seeking help with the concern I want to discuss?
  • What is your approach, and what might a first appointment involve?
  • Can you provide care where I will physically be located during sessions?
  • What appointment times and languages are currently available?
  • What happens if we decide a different therapist would be a better fit?
